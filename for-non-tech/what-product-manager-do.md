## 🔷 What Do Product Managers Actually Do?

Think of a Product Manager (PM) as the **"CEO of the product"** — not because they have authority over everyone, but because they are responsible for the success of the product across user needs, business goals, and tech feasibility.

They don’t typically code or design, but they:

---

### 🧭 1. Set the Product Vision and Strategy

- Define what the product should do and why it matters.
- Align product direction with business goals and user needs.

> **Example:**  
> _“We want to reduce customer churn by 20% through a better onboarding experience.”_

---

### 📋 2. Gather and Prioritize Requirements

- Collect feedback from users, customers, sales, support, etc.
- Prioritize features using frameworks (like **MoSCoW**, **RICE**, etc.).
- Write Product Requirements Documents (**PRDs**).

> **Example:**  
> _“Customers are asking for dark mode. Is this a quick win or a distraction?”_

---

### 🔁 3. Collaborate Across Teams

Work daily with:

- **Design** (to shape the UX)
- **Engineering** (to build it)
- **Marketing** (to launch it)
- **Sales/Support** (to explain it)

> _PMs translate what needs to be built and why, engineers figure out how._

---

### 🚀 4. Drive Product Development Lifecycle

- Run the roadmap, sprints, and feature launches.
- Make trade-off decisions (e.g., quality vs. deadline).
- Test features and iterate after user feedback.

> _They keep the train on track while adapting to what users need mid-journey._

---

### 📈 5. Measure Success

- Define and track KPIs (like adoption rate, NPS, conversion).
- Learn from failures or feature drops.
- Inform the next version of the product.

> _A feature is only done when it delivers real value to users._

---

### 🧠 So, in short

A Product Manager is a **decision-maker**, **communicator**, and **strategist** — focused on building the right thing, not just building things right.
